## Your core message

The role is **Manager, Business Development (Low Carbon)**, Lakewood, on-site, $140–180K. The job description centers on three jobs: selling 45Z tax credits, preparing CDR credits for issuance and sale, and modeling project economics.

**What the job description reveals.** Tallgrass is working with tax credit marketers to sell **45Z credits**, negotiating transfer agreements and buying credit insurance. It is also choosing **a preferred registry for CDR issuance**. So Tallgrass itself has, or expects to control, credits to sell. That updates our earlier assumption that all credits sit with the shippers. Ask about it (see Matt and JC questions).

**60-second pitch**, built from your résumé:

> "I've spent ten years structuring energy, commodity and low-carbon deals. Most recently, as Origination Manager at Compass International, I structured low-carbon fuel contracts across 12 projects, with $500M in intakes and $800M in offtakes. I built 45Q and CORC credits into those project economics through life cycle analysis, and modeled DCF, IRR and DSCR for investment committees. What draws me to Tallgrass is that Trailblazer is already running, so value now depends on commercial structure: which tax credit, which market credit, which registry. That is this role. I'd like to help sell 45Z well today and line up removal revenue for when 45Z ends in 2029."

**Three points to land:**

1. **You already think in this job's language:** 45Z versus 45Q, stacking, additionality, registry choice. Prove it with one sharp example, not a lecture.
2. **You can model it:** the job asks for DCF, ROI and profitability work. Have one example ready of a model you built and the decision it drove.
3. **You are honest with numbers:** say unprompted that BECCS averages of $330+ overstate what pure-CO2 ethanol tonnes will fetch. Managers hire people whose numbers they can trust.

- **Quoting $330–380/t as Trailblazer's price.** JC will know better. Say "BECCS averages are $330+, but pure-CO2 ethanol tonnes have realized $110–180."
